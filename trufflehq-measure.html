Most GTM measurement tracks The What. This tracks The How.

Quota attainment and pipeline coverage tell you what happened. Discovery quality, qualification rigor, and champion development tell you why, and predict what happens next. TruffleHQ's enablement function is designed to make both visible.

Why most measurement misses half the picture
The What is easy to measure. Salesforce tells you quota attainment, pipeline coverage, and win rate. Every revenue leader already tracks these. Enablement doesn't need to add them. It needs to connect its programs to them.
The How is what enablement actually changes. Whether a rep runs structured discovery. Whether they qualify to MEDDIC before moving a deal forward. Whether they develop a champion or just a contact. These behaviors drive The What outcomes, but they're invisible in most CRMs unless someone builds the infrastructure to capture them.
The gap is infrastructure, not intention. Revenue leaders want to know if enablement is working. Enablement wants to prove it. The problem is neither side built the data model that connects a training program to a deal outcome. TruffleHQ's measurement framework builds that connection deliberately, before programs launch, not as a retrospective report.
What TruffleHQ enablement measures
Time to first solo customer call
How fast does a new CSM reach independent readiness?
▼
Captured in
Salesforce: activity log filtered by account owner + activity type "Customer Call." Milestone date field set when manager completes Day 30 observation form gate.
What it proves
Ramp system works. Competency gates are producing ready reps faster than calendar-based onboarding did.
→ Connected to ramp program completion in LMS via Zapier
Win rate by enablement cohort
Do reps who completed GTM certification before their first deals close more?
▼
Captured in
Salesforce: closed/won data tagged with a cohort field set at hire. LMS certification completion date joined via Zapier to determine whether cert was complete before first deal closed.
What it proves
Certification investment has a measurable return. Or it does not. In which case the curriculum needs to change, not the measurement.
→ Reported monthly to revenue leadership in Salesforce dashboard
Renewal rate by CSM cohort
Does completing the full 90-day ramp show up in retention 12 months later?
▼
Captured in
Salesforce: renewal opportunity data tagged by CSM cohort. Ramp completion date from readiness rubric gate reviews logged as a custom field at hire.
What it proves
Ramp investment connects to long-term retention outcomes. A CSM who was rushed through onboarding will show up in renewal numbers within a year.
MEDDIC qualification score
Are reps actually qualifying before moving deals forward?
▼
Captured in
Salesforce: six custom checkbox fields, one per MEDDIC element. Each marked confirmed when the rep has documented evidence in the opportunity. Score = sum of confirmed elements (0–6).
What it proves
Whether MEDDIC discipline from the talk tracks and battlecard is translating into actual field behavior. Average score below 4 at pipeline review = qualification training gap, not a closing problem.
→ Call analysis can validate: AI call analysis flags whether discovery questions map to MEDDIC elements
Discovery quality score
Is the rep running structured discovery or improvising?
▼
Captured in
Relevant call analysis tool: AI call analysis scored against the TruffleHQ discovery track. Tracks whether reps asked impact questions, surfaced a specific cost, and confirmed a next step. Scores aggregated by rep and by cohort.
What it proves
Whether the discovery talk track changed behavior on calls. Reps trained on the track should score meaningfully higher than those who weren't. If they don't, the training needs to change.
→ Call analysis scorecards built to mirror the discovery talk track stages
Manager observation score at each ramp gate
Are reps demonstrating the right behaviors, not just completing the right tasks?
▼
Captured in
Salesforce custom object or Enablement platform tracker: manager observation forms at Day 30, 60, and 90 submit a structured score. Aggregated across cohorts to identify where the ramp system itself is producing weak results.
What it proves
Whether the readiness rubric is calibrating managers consistently. If scores vary wildly across managers for similar reps, the rubric needs tighter behavioral indicators, not more managers.
→ Zapier connects form submission to Salesforce cohort record automatically
Expansion pipeline by enablement participation
Does the expansion conversation training produce more pipeline?
▼
Captured in
Salesforce expansion opportunity data joined to LMS attendance records via Zapier. CSMs who attended the expansion conversation session compared to those who didn't, same tenure, same book size.
What it proves
Whether specific training programs move specific pipeline metrics. This is the argument enablement makes to revenue leadership when asking for time and resources.
MEDDIC qualification score
Deal quality at a glance
Built in Salesforce · 6 fields · 0–6 score
Each MEDDIC element is a checkbox field in Salesforce. A rep marks it confirmed when they have documented evidence, not when they think they know, but when it's captured in the opportunity record. Score aggregates automatically. Pipeline review uses the score to separate qualified opportunities from qualified-looking ones.

Why this matters for enablement: Average MEDDIC score across the pipeline is a direct readout of whether the battlecard and talk track training is working in the field. A score below 4 at pipeline review is not a closing problem. It is a qualification training problem. That distinction determines what gets built next.

What this framework doesn't claim. Correlation is not causation. A rep who completed certification and won more deals might have won those deals anyway. This framework surfaces patterns worth investigating, not proofs worth betting the roadmap on. Use the data to ask better questions, not to close the conversation.
