Intake to launch at GTM speed.

A lean enablement function can't afford a content development cycle that takes weeks. AI does the acceleration work: faster first drafts, smarter reformatting, gap detection against a checklist. Human judgment focuses on accuracy, tone, and the calls only people can make.

Request types
Type A · Highest priority
Strategic launch
Product launch, pricing change, major positioning update, competitive shift. Tied directly to annual GTM goals set by leadership.
Operationally significant. Changes how reps position, price, or handle a competitive objection. Distributes broadly and immediately.
Type B · Medium priority
Skill gap response
Pattern surfaced by relevant call analysis logs or pipeline data, not manager-reported. Recurring objection, qualification gap, drop-off at a specific deal stage.
Behavioral significance. Changes how reps handle a specific moment in the sales motion. Targeted to the affected cohort.
Type C · Planned
Evergreen refresh
Minor UI change, updated screenshot, terminology correction. Low operational impact. A button changed color, not a workflow.
Maintenance. Doesn't change rep behavior. Fits into the regular production cycle without interrupting higher-priority work.
Production pipeline
01
Intake and prioritization
Enablement · Human
▼
Requests enter from Product Marketing, Sales Leadership, CS, and RevOps. Each request is categorized by type and scored against two questions: does this connect to an annual GTM goal set by leadership, or is it an ad hoc need? And what is the operational significance: does this change how reps behave with customers, or is it a minor update with no behavioral impact. Those two filters determine how fast it moves and how broadly it distributes. A button changing color is not the same as a pricing structure changing. The pipeline treats them differently.

02
Context gathering
Enablement · Human
▼
Enablement reviews the relevant content before any drafting begins: positioning document, product release notes, customer customer objection logs, competitive intelligence update. For strategic launches, enablement is connected to product cycles and daily standups, not scheduling a separate sync after the fact. The goal is to surface the one or two things that most change how reps should talk about this product. That nuance rarely lives in a document.

03
AI-assisted drafting
Claude · AI
▼
A custom Claude prompt, trained on TruffleHQ's messaging framework, tone, and GTM competency model, generates the first draft. The prompt is built so outputs are anchored to TruffleHQ's language from the start, not requiring a full rewrite after the fact. Claude does three things in this step: produces a reviewable first draft faster than any human can, reformats existing content for different channels (battlecard becomes Slack update becomes certification question), and flags gaps against a checklist: missing objection handling, no competitive angle, no MEDDIC connection. What Claude does not do: make accuracy calls, assess competitive claims, or decide what the field actually needs to hear. That's the next step.

04
Human review gate
Enablement + SME · Human
▼
Enablement reviews for accuracy, tone, and field-readiness. A subject matter expert spot-checks any product claims or competitive comparisons. The question at review is not whether it looks good. It is whether a rep would use it exactly as written, and whether it would work. For Type A content, one round of review maximum. The cost of slow is higher than the cost of imperfect. Ship it, then refine based on what the field actually encounters.

05
Distribution and tracking
Enablement · Human
▼
The distribution decision is part of the design, not an afterthought. Slack for urgent competitive updates. Highspot for certification-required content. Salesforce for deal-stage-specific guidance that surfaces when a rep opens a relevant opportunity. Each piece is tagged in the tracking system so usage and adoption can be measured against the outcomes on the measure page.

On training Claude
The prompt is the infrastructure.
A generic Claude prompt produces generic content. A prompt trained on TruffleHQ's messaging framework, competency model, and tone produces content that sounds like TruffleHQ from the first draft. Building and maintaining that prompt is an enablement responsibility, not a one-time setup. As the product evolves, the messaging shifts, and the competitive landscape changes, the prompt gets updated. The prompt is a living document, not a configuration file.
