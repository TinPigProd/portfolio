Most organizations

TinPigProductions builds talent and learning infrastructure that holds both dimensions the performance that delivers results, and the behavior that makes those results sustainable.

01 · The problem

Why most talent
systems fail quietly.

Organizations invest in frameworks and get compliance. They invest in training and get completion rates. They run performance reviews and get ratings that reflect who managers like and how visible the work was. None of it changes behavior.

The gap isn't effort. Most people teams work hard. The gap is infrastructure. A competency framework that lives in a PDF is not infrastructure. A feedback conversation that produces vague, hedged observations is not development. An LMS that requires a manager to leave the flow of work to find a course is not in-platform enablement.

The systems exist. They just don't connect. And when they don't connect, the people in the middle managers trying to develop their teams, leaders trying to calibrate performance, employees trying to understand what's expected are left to fill the gaps with intuition and inconsistency.

The pattern
Framework exists. Behavior doesn't change.
Competency models are built, presented, and filed. They are not embedded in the conversations where they could actually change how managers observe, describe, and develop people.
The result
Calibration reflects visibility, not behavior.
Without shared behavioral language, performance decisions default to who presents well, who is loudest in the room, and who works directly with the people doing the rating.
02 · The framework

Performance is two things.
Most systems only measure one.

Every high-performing organization has people who deliver results and people who build the conditions that make results possible. These aren't always the same people but the best ones are both. The What and The How isn't a metaphor. It's a structural argument about what belongs in a competency framework and why.

The What captures performance delivery the behaviors that produce outcomes. Drives Results, Customer Focus, Decision Quality, Collaboration, Courage, Plans and Aligns. These are the enterprise-critical competencies that every role must demonstrate to function.

The How captures culture and growth the behaviors that make performance sustainable and organizations worth staying in. Learning Agility, Being Resilient, Develops Talent, Communicates Effectively, Instills Trust. These are the differentiating competencies that separate a team from a collection of individuals.

The What Performance delivery
Enterprise-critical competencies. What every role at every level must demonstrate to function. Anchored to organizational values. Observable in daily decisions, cross-functional behavior, and delivery against commitments.
The How Culture and growth
Culture-differentiating competencies. What makes someone distinctly valuable not just what they produce, but how they show up. These competencies predict retention, team performance, and readiness for the next level.
03 · The condition

The framework only works
if the culture makes it
safe to tell the truth.

This is the part most talent systems miss entirely. You can build the most rigorous competency framework in the world. You can embed it in every tool, every review, every calibration conversation. And it will still fail if the people using it don't feel safe being honest.

Leaders give shallow, hedged feedback not because they don't observe behavior, but because naming what they actually see feels risky. Shame fear of being wrong, of damaging a relationship, of being perceived as harsh produces exactly the kind of vague, unactionable feedback that leaves employees guessing and managers frustrated.

TinPigProductions's approach to talent infrastructure is informed by Brené Brown's research on shame, vulnerability, and psychological safety in organizational settings. The How competencies Instills Trust, Communicates Effectively, Learning Agility are not just behaviors to measure. They are the conditions that make honest measurement possible.

A manager who instills trust creates the environment where feedback is given and received as information, not judgment. An organization that curates curiosity creates the conditions where failure is data, not career risk. The framework and the culture are not separate investments. They are the same investment.

04 · The solution

Infrastructure that closes
the gap between knowing
and doing.

TinPigProductions doesn't build training. It builds the systems that make every talent touchpoint consistent, measurable, and honest from the interview question to the succession conversation.

The feedback synthesis tool takes raw manager observations and returns structured, competency-anchored insight. Not because the manager was inadequate but because translating what you observe into what someone can act on is genuinely hard, and a system can make it easier.

The succession tool replaces "not a team player" with a behavioral gap in Collaborates at L4. Not because the original observation was wrong but because behavioral language travels further, lands more cleanly, and creates a path forward rather than a verdict.

FlyingPig surfaces learning in Slack at the moment a feedback synthesis completes not a week later when the motivation has dissipated. Because the best learning infrastructure meets people where they are, in the tools they already use, at the moment when the insight is freshest.

The principle
Systems over events.
A training event changes knowledge. A system changes behavior. TinPigProductions builds the latter infrastructure that makes good behavior the path of least resistance rather than the exception.
The principle
In the flow, not adjacent to it.
Learning that requires leaving the work to go find it is learning that doesn't happen. TinPigProductions designs for the workflow that already exists, not for the ideal workflow that might exist someday.
